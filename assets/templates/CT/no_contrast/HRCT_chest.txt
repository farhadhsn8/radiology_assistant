HRCT chest
Technique – Thin section, non contrast high resolution CT images were acquired from the lung apices to the bases, with supine inspiratory, expiratory and prone inspiratory series. Mediastinal and lung window reconstructions were performed.
Findings
•	Cardiomediastinal structures: The heart, great vessels, trachea and oesophagus have a normal appearance. There is no mediastinal or hilar lymphadenopathy.
•	Pulmonary vessels and airways: Pulmonary arteries, veins and bronchi are of normal calibre; there is no bronchiectasis or airway wall thickening.
•	Pulmonary parenchyma: Lung parenchyma demonstrates normal interstitial markings. There are no nodules, ground glass opacities, consolidation, collapse, emphysema or cystic change. Expiratory and prone images show no air trapping. There is no focal pulmonary parenchymal or pleural abnormality and no thoracic lymphadenopathy.
•	Pleural spaces: No pleural effusion or pneumothorax; pleural spaces are clear.
•	Upper abdominal and bony structures: The liver, spleen, pancreas, adrenal glands and bowel segments seen at the lung bases have a normal non contrast appearance. Osseous structures of the spine, ribs and sternum are normal.
Impression – Normal high resolution chest CT with no evidence of interstitial lung disease or other intrathoracic pathology.
